The GPU is the [model serving](/system-design/ai-ml/model-serving-architectures)
tier's scarce, expensive, and *awkward* resource. Scarce (they cost
thousands to tens of thousands each, and there is a global shortage),
expensive (idle GPUs are the biggest line in an ML budget), and
awkward (they need to be *fed* — a GPU with no work in flight is
wasted, and moving data to it can cost more than the compute). The
management problem: **keep the accelerators busy without
oversubscribing their memory, across many models and many tenants.**
This is the [capacity planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
and [multi-tenancy](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
discipline applied to a device with its own memory hierarchy.

## The resource's shape

The GPU's parts, and what each means for scheduling:

- **Compute units** (the SM cores, in the thousands: the
  parallel work, what batching
  [saturates](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)).
- **Device memory** (the VRAM, tens to hundreds of GB: the *real*
  constraint — the model weights, the [KV cache](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)
  for LLMs, the intermediate activations all live here, and
  **out-of-memory is the failure mode** that shapes everything).
- **The memory bandwidth** (the bus between VRAM and the cores:
  often the actual bottleneck — a memory-bound layer waits on data
  movement, not compute, which is why
  [quantization](/system-design/ai-ml/inference-optimization)'s
  smaller weights help *twice*).
- **The host link** (PCIe/NVLink between the CPU's RAM and the
  GPU's: moving a model *in* is slow, which is the
  [cold-start](/system-design/ai-ml/serverless-inference) and
  [warm-pool](/system-design/ai-ml/model-caching-warm-pools) whole
  problem).

## The scheduling patterns

How many models, on how many GPUs, in what arrangement:

- **One model per GPU** (the simple, wasteful default): the model
  in VRAM, the GPU dedicated. Utilization is *low* (a model at
  moderate traffic does not saturate the GPU), and the fix is
  batching or sharing — but it is the *correct* answer when the
  model is large (the [LLM](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)
  case: the weights + KV cache fill the GPU) or when the tenant
  requires isolation.
- **Many models per GPU** (MPS, MIG, or a serving framework's
  multi-model mode): share the device — **MIG** (Multi-Instance GPU:
  hardware-partitioned slices, strong isolation, fixed sizes) or
  **MPS** (software sharing: better packing, weaker isolation), or
  the framework's own model-multiplexing (load/unload as traffic
  demands: the [warm-pool](/system-design/ai-ml/model-caching-warm-pools)
  dial). The trade: **packing** (higher utilization: many small
  models on one GPU) against **isolation** (a noisy model, or a
  crash, affecting its neighbors — the
  [multi-tenant](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
  containment, again).
- **One model across many GPUs** (the *large*-model case: a model
  that does not fit one GPU's VRAM, split across several:
  tensor/pipeline parallelism, and the *interconnect* (NVLink,
  InfiniBand) becomes the bottleneck, which is a
  [distributed-training-style](/system-design/ai-ml/inference-optimization)
  problem appearing at *inference* time).

## The utilization problem

The number that governs the whole design: **GPU utilization** (the
fraction of time the device is doing useful work), and the honest
arithmetic that idle GPUs are the dominant cost. The levers, in the
order to apply them:

1. **Batch** (the biggest win: [batching](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)
   turns a memory-bound single forward pass into a compute-bound
   batch, raising utilization dramatically).
2. **Quantize** (smaller weights: more models per GPU, less
   bandwidth-bound — [the optimization
   page](/system-design/ai-ml/inference-optimization)).
3. **Share** (many small models per GPU: MPS/MIG, or
   load/unload multiplexing for the long tail of rarely-used models).
4. **Scale to zero / spot** (the [serverless](/system-design/ai-ml/serverless-inference)
   answer for bursty or experimental workloads, and the
   [spot-instance](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
   economics for interruptible batch).
5. **Right-size** (the model that needs a fraction of an A100 on a
   fraction of an A100: the [distillation/pruning
   family](/system-design/ai-ml/inference-optimization) making the
   model fit the cheaper device).

## The operational shape

The GPU fleet is scheduled by an orchestrator with GPU awareness
(Kubernetes with device plugins, or a dedicated scheduler), and the
ML-specific scheduling concerns: **gang scheduling** (a model split
across GPUs needs *all* its GPUs at once, not half — the
all-or-nothing resource request), **preemption** (the
[spot](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
and priority classes: batch training yields to serving), and
**topology awareness** (which GPUs are on the same NVLink island:
the [placement](/system-design/fundamentals/consistent-hashing)
question, at hardware scale). The
[observability](/system-design/fundamentals/observability) is
GPU-specific: utilization, memory, and the **memory-fragmentation**
metric (a GPU that is "full" of fragments but has free bytes:
the reason LLM serving's [paged attention](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)
exists).
