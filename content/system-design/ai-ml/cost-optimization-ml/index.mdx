---
title: "Cost Optimization for ML"
summary: "The training and serving bill: where it goes and how to spend less."
---

ML has two bills — **training** (GPUs by the hour, for hours to
weeks, per experiment) and **serving** (GPUs or CPUs by the
request, forever) — and the discipline of *cost optimization* is
understanding which dominates (it varies: a research-heavy org
spends on training, a product-heavy one on serving), where the
waste is, and which lever addresses it. This is the
[capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
and [cost-efficiency](/system-design/fundamentals/scalability)
discipline applied to ML's two expensive resources: *accelerator
time* and *tokens*.

## Where the money goes

- **Training: accelerator-hours** (the [distributed
  training](/system-design/ai-ml/distributed-training) fleet,
  times the number of runs (the
  [hyperparameter-tuning](/system-design/ai-ml/hyperparameter-tuning)
  and [experiment](/system-design/ai-ml/experiment-tracking)
  churn), and the *idle* time (GPUs waiting for data, for
  checkpoints, for a scheduler: the utilization gap is often the
  biggest line).
- **Serving: GPU-hours (or CPU-hours) and tokens** (the
  [serving tier's](/system-design/ai-ml/model-serving-architectures)
  always-on cost, dominated by *utilization* (an idle GPU is pure
  waste: the [batching](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)
  and [caching](/system-design/ai-ml/model-caching-warm-pools)
  levers) and, for LLMs, the per-token bill (the
  [cost-budget](/system-design/ai-ml/cost-token-budgets) page)).
- **Storage and data movement** (the datasets, checkpoints, and
  artifacts ([object storage](/system-design/fundamentals/object-storage)),
  plus the egress and the data-transfer between training nodes: the
  [capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
  line items, usually smaller than the compute but not free).

## The training levers

- **Right-size the accelerator** (a small model does not need an
  A100: the [GPU-management](/system-design/ai-ml/gpu-accelerator-management)
  matching, and the [distillation](/system-design/ai-ml/inference-optimization)
  that makes the model fit a cheaper device).
- **Spot/preemptible instances** (training is *interruptible* (with
  checkpoints): the spot discount (often 50-70%) is the single
  biggest training lever, at the cost of the
  [fault-tolerance](/system-design/ai-ml/distributed-training)
  machinery (checkpoint, resume, reconfigure) — the trade the
  [job scheduler](/system-design/hld/distributed-job-scheduler)
  handles).
- **Kill losing runs early** (the [early
  pruning](/system-design/ai-ml/hyperparameter-tuning) discipline:
  most trials lose, and the saving is *killing them before they
  finish* — the [successive-halving](/system-design/ai-ml/hyperparameter-tuning)
  machinery, and the biggest *tuning* saving).
- **Improve utilization** (the GPU waits for data: the *input
  pipeline* (the [data-loader](/system-design/fundamentals/stream-vs-batch-processing)
  feeding the accelerator) is a common bottleneck, and fixing it
  (prefetching, faster storage, better sharding) is *free* compute —
  the utilization gap, closed).

## The serving levers

- **Utilize the GPU** ([batching](/system-design/ai-ml/batching-and-latency)
  (the biggest), [quantization](/system-design/ai-ml/inference-optimization)
  (smaller = more per GPU), and the
  [cache](/system-design/ai-ml/model-caching-warm-pools): the
  utilization ladder, and the reason an "expensive" GPU serving a
  batched model is *cheaper per request* than a "cheap" one serving
  one-at-a-time).
- **Route by cost** (the [gateway's](/system-design/ai-ml/llm-gateways)
  difficulty routing: cheap model for easy requests — the single
  biggest *LLM* serving lever).
- **Cache aggressively** (the [LLM caches](/system-design/ai-ml/llm-caching):
  the zero-cost request).
- **Scale to zero (where possible)** (the
  [long tail](/system-design/ai-ml/serverless-inference): pay only
  for what is used — with the [cold-start](/system-design/ai-ml/serverless-inference)
  caveat).
- **Right-size the fleet** (the [autoscaling](/system-design/ai-ml/autoscaling-inference)
  discipline: not over-provisioned, not under — the headroom
  arithmetic).

## The organizational view

The honest systems lesson: **cost is a *first-class metric*, and
making it visible is most of the optimization.** The org that
*measures* cost per experiment, per model, per feature, per tenant
(the [observability](/system-design/fundamentals/observability)
discipline, applied to money) finds the waste (the idle GPUs, the
runaway [agent](/system-design/ai-ml/agents-and-tool-use) loops,
the over-provisioned fleet, the
[duplicated experiments](/system-design/ai-ml/experiment-tracking))
because it is *on a dashboard*, and the org that does not
discovers it at the end of the quarter, when the bill arrives. The
[unit economics](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
(cost per prediction, cost per resolved ticket, cost per trained
model) is the metric that turns ML spending from an opaque
line-item into an *engineered* quantity — which is the whole
discipline, in one sentence.
