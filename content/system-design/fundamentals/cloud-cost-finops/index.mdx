---
title: "Cloud Cost & FinOps"
summary: "The cloud bill as an engineering constraint: where it goes and how to reduce it."
---

The cloud turned infrastructure into an *operating expense*, which
sounds like an improvement and introduces a new engineering problem:
**the bill is proportional to how you use the system, and nothing in
the code tells you the cost.** A design that is elegant and correct
can be ten times more expensive than an equally correct one, and the
difference is invisible until the invoice arrives. **FinOps** is the
discipline of making cost *visible and engineerable*: measuring it,
attributing it, and reducing it — the
[capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
mindset applied to money.

## Where the money goes

The cloud bill decomposes into a few large buckets, and knowing which
one dominates a system is the first step:

- **Compute** (VMs, containers, serverless invocations: the largest
  line for most systems — and the one where *utilization* is the
  lever: an idle instance is pure waste, the
  [utilization](/ai-ml/systems/gpu-accelerator-management)
  argument).
- **Storage** (object storage, disks, databases: usually cheap per
  GB but grows forever — the [retention](/system-design/fundamentals/object-storage)
  policy is a *cost* decision).
- **Network/egress** (data leaving the cloud, and *cross-region*
  traffic: the surprising one — egress is often far pricier than
  people expect, and a chatty multi-region design pays for every
  byte crossing a boundary: the [multi-region](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-region-and-active-active)
  cost dimension).
- **Managed services** (databases, queues, caches, and per-request
  pricing: convenient, and priced at a premium — the
  [build-vs-buy](/system-design/fundamentals/microservices-vs-monolith)
  decision, in dollars).

## The levers

The cost levers, in the order to reach for them:

- **Right-size** (the most common waste: instances bigger than the
  workload needs. The [capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
  arithmetic tells you what you *need*, and the gap to what you
  *bought* is the waste).
- **Improve utilization** (batch jobs on
  [spot/preemptible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preemptible_virtual_machine)
  instances (50-70% cheaper, at the fault-tolerance cost — the
  [distributed-training](/ai-ml/systems/distributed-training)
  checkpoint discipline), autoscaling to match demand (the
  [autoscaling](/system-design/fundamentals/scalability) discipline),
  and packing workloads onto fewer, fuller machines).
- **Cache and absorb** (the [CDN](/system-design/fundamentals/cdn)
  and [cache](/system-design/fundamentals/caching) layers reduce
  *both* latency and compute/egress: the rare lever that improves
  everything).
- **Delete** (the cheapest optimization: unused resources,
  forgotten instances, orphaned volumes, over-long
  [retention](/system-design/fundamentals/object-storage) — the
  "just turn it off" that is embarrassingly often the biggest win).
- **Choose the tier** (serverless for spiky/low-traffic (the
  [serverless](/ai-ml/systems/serverless-inference) economics),
  reserved/committed capacity for steady-state, spot for
  interruptible — matching the pricing model to the workload shape).

## The discipline: make cost visible

The organizational half, and the reason FinOps is a *practice*: **cost
must be a metric, on a dashboard, attributed to a team/service.** The
machinery: **tagging** (every resource tagged with its owner/service,
so the bill is *attributable*), **showback/chargeback** (teams see —
and ideally pay — their share: the incentive that changes behavior,
the [multi-tenant](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenant-ml-platforms)
cost-attribution idea), **unit economics** (cost per request, per
user, per prediction: the [capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
metric that makes cost *comparable to value*), and **budgets and
alerts** (a spend anomaly pages, like any
[SLO](/system-design/fundamentals/reliability-engineering) — the
"why did the bill triple overnight" catch).

The engineering payoff: **the same visibility that finds waste also
makes cost a *design input*** — "this design costs 3x that one" is a
trade-off the team can *see*, and a change that adds cost is a
decision, not a surprise. Cost becomes another dimension of the
[capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
conversation, alongside latency and throughput.

## The honest trade

The counterweight worth stating: **premature cost optimization is as
bad as premature performance optimization.** Over-engineering for
cost (spot instances for a critical service, aggressive downsizing
that hurts reliability, hand-rolled infrastructure to avoid a managed
fee) trades a *small, predictable* bill for *large, unpredictable*
risk — and the mature answer is the
[capacity-planning](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
one: *measure first*, find the dominant cost, optimize *that*, and
accept the rest as the price of the design. The bill is a signal, not
a mandate.
