Image classification is the foundational computer-vision task: given
an image, assign it a label ("cat", "dog", "tumor", "defective"). It
is the task that deep learning first dominated, the benchmark that
drove the [CNN](/ai-ml/concepts/convolutional-neural-networks)
revolution, and still the workhorse behind a huge fraction of
production vision — content moderation, medical imaging, quality
inspection, photo organization. Understanding it (and its evolution)
makes the rest of vision legible.

## The task and the pipeline

Input: an image (a grid of pixels). Output: a label (or a probability
over labels). The pipeline: an image is fed through a network that
extracts increasingly abstract features (edges → textures → parts →
objects) and ends with a classifier over the labels. The training
signal is labeled examples (this image is a cat), and the model learns
the features that separate the classes — the
[supervised](/ai-ml/systems/supervised-unsupervised-rl) setup.

The challenges that make it non-trivial: **intra-class variation**
(the same object looks different from different angles, in different
lighting, at different scales), **inter-class similarity** (a wolf and
a dog look alike), **occlusion**, and **context** (a small object
might need its surroundings to classify). Deep networks handle these
by learning *invariant* features — the reason
[parameter sharing and hierarchy](/ai-ml/concepts/convolutional-neural-networks)
matter.

## The evolution

- **Hand-crafted features** (pre-deep-learning: humans designed edge
  and texture detectors — SIFT, HOG — and fed them to a classifier).
- **The CNN breakthrough** (AlexNet, 2012: a deep
  [CNN](/ai-ml/concepts/convolutional-neural-networks) trained on a
  large dataset crushed the hand-crafted approach, launching the deep
  learning era. Then VGG, ResNet (with
  [residual connections](/ai-ml/concepts/normalization-and-initialization)),
  and efficient architectures pushed accuracy up and cost down).
- **Transfer learning** (the practical shift: instead of training from
  scratch, [fine-tune](/ai-ml/concepts/transfer-learning) a
  pre-trained image model on your small dataset — the reason image
  classification is now accessible to anyone with a few hundred
  labeled images).
- **Vision transformers** (splitting an image into patches and applying
  the [transformer](/ai-ml/concepts/transformers) architecture — now
  competitive with or better than CNNs at scale).
- **Zero-shot classification** (models like
  [CLIP](/ai-ml/concepts/vision-language-models) classify into
  *arbitrary* label sets given as text, with no task-specific
  training — a genuinely new capability).

## What it means in production

Image classification is often the *simplest* vision capability to ship
and a component of harder ones
([detection and segmentation](/ai-ml/concepts/object-detection-and-segmentation)
build on classification-like features). The engineering concerns are
the usual ML-systems ones: the data (labeled examples, class balance,
distribution shift), the model size versus latency (running on a
phone or a server — [edge inference](/ai-ml/systems/edge-on-device-inference)),
and monitoring for drift as the real world diverges from training. The
concept is the entry point to vision; the evolution (hand-crafted →
CNN → transformer → zero-shot) is a compact history of modern AI.
