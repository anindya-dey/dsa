Between the weighted sums of a neural network sit the **activation
functions**: small non-linear functions applied to each neuron's
output. They look like a detail and they are actually the *reason
neural networks can learn anything interesting*: without them, a stack
of layers would collapse into a single linear function, and depth
would buy nothing. The choice of activation is a real design decision
with real consequences for training.

## Why non-linearity is the whole point

Consider two layers with no activation between them:
`output = W₂ · (W₁ · x) = (W₂W₁) · x`, a *single* linear
transformation. Any number of linear layers compose into one linear
layer, so a deep network without activations is just a
[linear model](/ai-ml/concepts/linear-and-logistic-regression) with
extra steps. The activation breaks that: it makes each layer's output
a *non-linear* function of its input, so composing layers composes
non-linearities, and the network can represent curves, interactions,
and arbitrarily complex patterns. **The activation is what makes
"deep" mean anything.**

## The functions, and their history

- **Sigmoid / tanh** (the early choices): smooth S-shaped curves that
  squash the output into a range (`(0,1)` or `(-1,1)`). Intuitive
  (they look like a neuron "firing"), and problematic in deep
  networks: their gradients *saturate* (become near-zero at the
  extremes), so gradients **vanish** as they back-propagate through
  many layers, and deep networks barely train. This was a major
  reason early deep learning stalled.
- **ReLU** (`max(0, x)`, the modern default): output the input if
  positive, else zero. Dead simple, and it *fixed* the vanishing
  gradient problem (the gradient is 1 for positive inputs, so it does
  not shrink through depth), which is why the ReLU era unlocked deep
  networks. Its issue: **dying ReLUs** (a neuron stuck outputting
  zero for all inputs stops learning, the fix is variants).
- **ReLU variants** (Leaky ReLU, ELU, GELU, SiLU): tweaks that keep a
  small gradient for negative inputs (avoiding dead neurons) or
  smooth the curve. **GELU and SiLU** are the modern defaults in
  [transformers](/ai-ml/concepts/transformers) and large models.
- **Softmax** (the output layer for classification): turns a vector
  of scores into a *probability distribution* (all positive, summing
  to 1), which is why classifiers end in softmax + cross-entropy.

## The design considerations

- **The output activation matches the task** (softmax for
  multi-class, sigmoid for binary, *none* (linear) for regression,
a common mistake is applying an activation to a regression output,
  which clips the predictions to a range).
- **Hidden activations affect trainability** (the ReLU family for
  hidden layers, sigmoid/tanh only where their squashing is wanted).
- **Smoothness matters for some architectures** (the smooth
  activations (GELU/SiLU) help in very deep models and transformers;
  ReLU is fine and faster elsewhere).

## The takeaway

The activation function is a one-line detail with an outsized effect:
it is the source of a network's expressive power, and its *gradient
behavior* is a major factor in whether a deep network trains at all.
The shift from sigmoid/tanh to the ReLU family is one of the quiet
reasons deep learning took off.
