---
title: "Fine-Tuning vs Prompting vs RAG"
summary: "The three ways to change a model's behavior, their costs, and how to choose."
---

When a base model does not do what you need, there are exactly
**three** ways to change its behavior, and choosing among them is
one of the most consequential (and most-fumbled) decisions in
applied LLM work:

- **Prompting**: change the *input* (instructions, examples,
  retrieved context).
- **RAG**: change the *context* (retrieve and inject facts, the
  [RAG page](/ai-ml/systems/rag)'s subject).
- **Fine-tuning**: change the *weights* (train the model further on
  your data, the [LoRA page](/ai-ml/systems/lora-peft)'s
  subject).

They solve *different* problems, and the single most common mistake
is reaching for fine-tuning to fix a problem that prompting or RAG
solves better and cheaper. This page is the decision framework.

## What each actually changes

| | Changes | Good at | Bad at | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prompting** | the input text | format, style, tone, reasoning steps, task framing | injecting *knowledge*, guaranteeing behavior | ~free, instant |
| **RAG** | the context (facts in the prompt) | **knowledge**: private, fresh, factual grounding | teaching *skills* or *style*, latency/cost of retrieval | retrieval infra |
| **Fine-tuning** | the weights | **behavior**: format adherence, domain style, a specific skill, smaller models that punch up | injecting *facts* (they get memorized, go stale), cheap iteration | training run + serving |

The clean mental split: **prompting and RAG change what the model
*sees*; fine-tuning changes what the model *is*.** Knowledge is a
"what it sees" problem (RAG: inject the facts, no training); behavior
and skill are a "what it is" problem (fine-tuning: bake in the
pattern). And the recurring trap: **fine-tuning to add knowledge**
(the facts get memorized unreliably, go stale when they change, and
you cannot cite them, RAG's job), and **prompting to fix a
capability the model lacks** (no wording teaches a model a skill it
does not have, fine-tuning's job).

## The decision ladder

The order to *try* the options, cheapest and fastest first:

1. **Prompting** (can a better instruction or a few-shot example do
   it?): free, instant, and it *frequently* works, the first step,
   always. (The [prompt-as-artifact](/ai-ml/systems/prompt-engineering-systems)
   discipline applies: test the prompt change.)
2. **RAG** (is it a *knowledge* problem: private data, fresh data,
   verifiable citations?): the [RAG](/ai-ml/systems/rag)
   pipeline, and the answer for "the model does not know my stuff."
3. **Fine-tuning** (is it a *behavior/skill/format* problem
   prompting cannot crack, or do you need a *smaller/cheaper* model
   that performs like a bigger one?): the [LoRA/PEft](/ai-ml/systems/lora-peft)
   path, and note the *last* reason: **distillation** (fine-tune a
   small model on a big model's outputs, so you *serve* the small
   one: the [inference-optimization](/ai-ml/systems/inference-optimization)
   motivation, and a very common real reason to fine-tune).

The ladder is *cheapest-first* because each rung is slower and more
expensive to iterate: a prompt change ships in minutes, a RAG change
in hours, a fine-tune in days. Iterating at the cheapest rung that
solves the problem is the whole discipline.

## The combinations (they compose)

The three are not exclusive, and the mature systems *combine* them:

- **Fine-tuned model + RAG** (the common production stack: fine-tune
  for the *behavior* (answer format, domain tone, instruction-
  following), RAG for the *knowledge*, each doing what it is good
  at, the model shaped to *use* retrieved context well).
- **Prompt + RAG** (the most common: a well-engineered prompt that
  constrains generation *plus* retrieval for facts: the
  [RAG page's](/ai-ml/systems/rag) "answer only from the
  context" instruction, prompt-engineered).
- **Distillation** (fine-tune a small model on a large model's
  outputs: the *serving-cost* combination, trading a training run
  for a cheaper fleet).

## The honest costs of fine-tuning

Because fine-tuning is the rung people over-reach for, its costs,
stated plainly: a **training pipeline** (data curation, the
[lineage](/ai-ml/systems/data-versioning-lineage), the compute:
the [MLOps](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics) machinery),
a **serving cost** (you now serve *your* model: the
[serving tier](/ai-ml/systems/model-serving-architectures),
the [LoRA routing](/ai-ml/systems/lora-peft)), a
**maintenance burden** (the base model improves, and your fine-tune
is now behind, the re-fine-tuning cadence: the
[drift](/ai-ml/systems/concept-drift-retraining) problem, at
the model-release scale), and a **loss of generality** (a
narrowly-fine-tuned model can get *worse* at everything else: the
catastrophic-forgetting risk, mitigated by
[LoRA](/ai-ml/systems/lora-peft)'s light touch). Each is a
reason to climb the ladder slowly.
