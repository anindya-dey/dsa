title: "Evaluating LLM Systems"
summary: "LLM-as-judge, groundedness checks, and the honest measurement of open-ended generation."
---

Evaluating a classifier is [solved-ish](/ai-ml/systems/evaluation-metrics):
a held-out set, a metric, a number. Evaluating *generation* is
genuinely hard, because the output is *open-ended* (there is no
single right answer to "summarize this," so exact-match metrics are
meaningless), *long* (a paragraph's quality is not one number), and
*subjective* (helpfulness, tone, correctness are judgments). The
field's answers — **LLM-as-judge**, **groundedness checks**, and
**human evaluation** — are each a *proxy* with a known bias, and
this page is the honest accounting of the proxies and their
calibration.

## The metric families

- **Reference-based metrics** (BLEU, ROUGE: n-gram overlap with a
  reference answer — *cheap, automatic, and weak*: they reward
  surface similarity, not correctness, and a *better* answer that
  words things differently scores worse: useful for narrow tasks
  (translation, extractive summary) and misleading for open
  generation).
- **Embedding-based similarity** (semantic similarity to a
  reference: better than n-gram overlap (paraphrase-friendly),
  still blind to *correctness* (a confidently-wrong answer can be
  semantically *close* to the reference) — a proxy, used with
  awareness).
- **LLM-as-judge** (the modern default: a *strong model* scores the
  output against criteria ("is this helpful, accurate, and on-
  policy?"), with a rubric — the *scalable* quality metric, because
  it approximates human judgment at machine cost, and it is the
  reason LLM evaluation is *possible* at scale at all).
- **Task-specific checks** (the *honest* ones: does the JSON parse
  (the [guardrails](/ai-ml/systems/llm-guardrails-validation)
  check), does the [RAG](/ai-ml/systems/rag) answer cite a
  retrieved chunk (groundedness), did the [agent's](/ai-ml/systems/agents-and-tool-use)
  action *actually* succeed (the side-effect verification) — the
  checks that measure the *thing the system needed*, not a proxy for
  quality).
- **Human evaluation** (the ground truth, expensive and slow: the
  *calibration set* — a small, carefully-judged sample that
  *validates* the automatic metrics, the
  [offline/online](/ai-ml/systems/offline-vs-online-evaluation)
  discipline's anchor).

## LLM-as-judge, honestly

The technique that made LLM eval tractable, and its biases — which
*must* be corrected for, or the eval is noise:

- **Position bias** (the judge prefers the first (or second) answer:
  the fix is to *swap positions* and average — the standard
  protocol).
- **Verbosity bias** (the judge prefers *longer* answers: a real,
  documented preference, corrected by explicit rubrics or length
  normalization).
- **Self-preference** (the judge prefers answers from *its own
  family*: a judge should differ from the model under test, and the
  bias is measured and noted).
- **The calibration requirement** (the judge's scores must *agree
  with humans* on the calibration set — a judge that does not
  correlate with human judgment is measuring itself: the
  *judge-quality* metric, and the reason "we use an LLM judge" is
  not the end of the conversation).

## Groundedness: the RAG-specific evaluation

For [RAG](/ai-ml/systems/rag) (and any retrieval-grounded
system), the metrics that matter are *retrieval* and *grounding*,
and they are measured *separately* (because they fail
independently):

- **Retrieval metrics** (did the pipeline fetch the *relevant*
  chunks? precision/recall of the retrieval stage, measured against
  a labeled query→chunk set: the
  [chunking](/ai-ml/systems/chunking-indexing-strategies)
  and [embedding](/ai-ml/systems/embeddings-vector-representations)
  quality, isolated).
- **Groundedness / faithfulness** (does the answer *follow from* the
  retrieved context, or hallucinate beyond it? measured by an
  LLM-judge with the context as evidence, or by citation-checking:
  the *generation* quality, isolated).
- **Answer relevance** (does it answer the *question*?): the third
  axis, and separating all three is what makes a RAG failure
  *diagnosable* (retrieval miss? grounding failure? off-topic? —
  the [failure taxonomy](/ai-ml/systems/rag), measured).

## The evaluation system

The machinery, assembled: the **eval set** (curated inputs + expected
behaviors, versioned, growing with each incident — the
[regression suite](/ai-ml/systems/prompt-engineering-systems)
for the whole LLM system), the **judge pipeline** (the rubric, the
judge model, the bias corrections, the calibration against humans),
the **per-stage metrics** (retrieval, grounding, format, safety,
latency, cost — the *decomposed* scorecard that localizes failures),
and the **online tie-in** (the [A/B](/ai-ml/systems/ab-testing-experimentation)
and guardrail metrics that close the loop to real users — the
[offline/online](/ai-ml/systems/offline-vs-online-evaluation)
funnel, LLM-shaped). The honest summary: LLM evaluation is *proxy
management* — every metric is a biased estimate, the discipline is
knowing each bias, calibrating against humans, and never treating a
proxy as the truth.
