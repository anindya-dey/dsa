AutoML is [hyperparameter tuning](/ai-ml/systems/hyperparameter-tuning)
generalized: instead of searching the hyperparameters of *one*
chosen model, search the *model choice itself* (which algorithm,
which architecture, which features, which preprocessing), the
attempt to automate the *data scientist's* decisions, not just the
tuning. The promise ("throw data in, get a model out") and the
reality (it helps *somewhere* and misleads elsewhere) are both
worth understanding, because AutoML's honest value is a *specific*
niche, not a replacement for modeling.

## What it searches

- **Model selection** (try many algorithms (linear, trees, GBDTs,
  small nets) on the data and pick the best: the *ensemble* of
  candidates, automated, and often *surprisingly effective*, because
  for tabular data a well-tuned GBDT beats a fancy architecture
  more often than not, and AutoML finds that out).
- **Feature engineering** (generate and select features
  automatically: interactions, encodings, aggregations, the
  [feature-engineering](/ai-ml/systems/feature-engineering-basics)
  work, automated, with the *leakage risk* that automated feature
  generation can manufacture a leak the eval set does not catch).
- **Architecture search (NAS)** (search the *neural architecture*
  (layers, connections, sizes): the expensive, research-heavy
  version (each candidate is a training run), and the one whose
  gains are *hardest* to justify (a NAS-discovered architecture
  often barely beats a hand-designed one, at a large search cost,
the honest verdict for most applications).
- **Pipeline search** (the whole pipeline: preprocessing + features
  + model + postprocessing, the "automate the data scientist"
  ambition, and the least reliable, because the pipeline's *joins
  and splits* are where the [leakage](/ai-ml/systems/training-data-pipelines)
  hides, and automated pipelines are *good at accidentally
  leaking*).

## Where it actually helps

The honest niche, and the page's real content:

- **Tabular problems with clear metrics** (the classic AutoML win:
  a churn/risk/propensity problem, a labeled dataset, an offline
  metric, AutoML searches the model space and returns a
  competitive baseline *fast*, freeing the data scientist for the
  parts AutoML cannot do: the *features* and the *problem framing*).
- **Baselines and speed** (AutoML as the *first* model (the
  baseline to beat): the [experiment-tracking](/ai-ml/systems/experiment-tracking)
  loop, automated, giving a number in hours rather than weeks).
- **The long tail of models** (the many small models a platform
  hosts: AutoML generates the baseline for each, and a human
  improves only the ones that matter, the
  [cost-optimization](/ai-ml/systems/cost-optimization-ml)
  leverage: automated baselines for the tail, human effort for the
  head).

And where it *misleads*: **the metric is not the goal** (AutoML
optimizes the *specified* metric: if the metric is wrong (a proxy,
a leakage-prone split, an aggregate hiding a
[segment](/ai-ml/systems/bias-fairness-explainability)), AutoML
optimizes the wrong thing *efficiently*, the [Goodhart's
law](/ai-ml/systems/evaluation-metrics) problem, automated);
**the problem framing is not automated** (what to predict, which
data is legitimate, what "good" means: the decisions that *matter*
are the ones AutoML does not make); and **the production pipeline
is not automated** (a model that wins offline still needs the
[serving](/ai-ml/systems/model-serving-architectures),
[monitoring](/ai-ml/systems/model-monitoring-drift), and
[retraining](/ai-ml/systems/concept-drift-retraining)
machinery, AutoML produces a *model*, not a *system*).

## The systems shape

AutoML is the [tuning controller](/ai-ml/systems/hyperparameter-tuning)
with a *larger* search space, so it inherits the tuning page's
machinery (many parallel trials, early pruning, the budget
arithmetic) and adds *more* trials (the space is bigger: model
choice × features × hyperparameters), which makes the *pruning*
and the *budget* even more critical (an unpruned AutoML search is
the most expensive way to find a mediocre model). The mature
framing: AutoML is a *tool in the pipeline* (the baseline generator,
the tail-model factory), *not a strategy*, and the org that
treats it as "the data scientist replacement" gets efficient
optimization of whatever they failed to specify, which is the
[responsible-AI](/ai-ml/systems/responsible-ai-safety)
governance concern wearing an automation costume.
