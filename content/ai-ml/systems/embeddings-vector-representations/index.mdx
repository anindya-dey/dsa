---
title: "Embeddings & Vector Representations"
summary: "Meaning as geometry: representing items as dense vectors so similarity becomes distance, and the systems that store and compare them."
---

An embedding is a **vector that represents the meaning of a thing**:
a word, a product, a user, a sentence, an image, mapped to a point
in a high-dimensional space (say, 384 to 3000 dimensions) such that
**the geometry encodes semantics**: things that are *similar* land
*near* each other, and "near" is measured with a distance metric
(cosine similarity or dot product, almost always). This single
trick, meaning as position, is the foundation of the modern era:
[semantic search](/system-design/fundamentals/search-and-inverted-index),
[RAG](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system), the
[recommendation](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system)'s
candidate retrieval, "more like this", clustering, and the entire
vector-database industry. And it is a *systems* topic because the
representation creates a specific infrastructure: **billions of
points, compared by distance, in milliseconds.**

## What the vector is, and how it's made

The embedding is *learned*: a model (a
[transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_(deep_learning_architecture))
text encoder, a
[word2vec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word2vec)-class model, a
two-tower retrieval model, a
[contrastive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrastive_learning)
scorer) trained so that "similar-in-training" things map to
near-in-space points: trained on co-occurrence ("these words appear
in similar contexts"), on co-engagement ("users who clicked both"),
on labels ("these images were tagged alike"). What comes out is a
*function*: thing → vector, and the function's training data
decides what "similarity" *means* (an embedding trained on
encyclopedic text makes "bank" similar to "finance"; trained on
fishing forums, similar to "river": **there is no neutral
embedding**, the space encodes its training corpus's semantics,
which is a design decision, not a library import).

The engineering surface: the **embedding model is deployed
infrastructure** (the encoder itself runs as a
[serving](/ai-ml/systems/batch-vs-real-time-prediction) path:
documents embedded at ingest, queries embedded at search), the
**vectors are precomputed artifacts** (the items' embeddings
computed once at ingest, stored: the
[feature store](/ai-ml/systems/feature-stores)'s offline
tier, the same pattern), and the **query-side embedding is the
latency budget** (encode the query, 10-50ms with a small
[model](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics), then
compare against precomputed vectors: the
[autocomplete](/system-design/hld/search-autocomplete) design's
shape, with meaning instead of prefixes).

## The distance metrics

How "near" is measured, and why the choice matters:

- **Cosine similarity** (the default for text embeddings: the
  *angle* between vectors, ignoring magnitude): robust to scale
  differences, and what most
  [embedding models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_embedding)
  are trained to optimize, so "use cosine unless the model says
  otherwise" is the standing rule.
- **Dot product** (the fast cousin: unnormalized vectors, magnitude
  matters): what the ANN indexes *actually compute* when the
  vectors are normalized (the two are *identical* on unit vectors,
  so "cosine similarity, implemented as dot product over
  normalized vectors" is the standard stack, and the
  normalization is a real design step, not a detail).
- **Euclidean (L2)** (straight distance): the default for
  [image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_retrieval) and
  [tabular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feature_vector) spaces,
  and the metric the [geospatial](/system-design/fundamentals/geospatial-indexing)
  family uses (the nearest-neighbor question, worn by different
  data).

The design conversation the metric creates: *what is the model's
similarity trained to be* (semantic equivalence? engagement
overlap? visual nearness?), and the metric must match the training
(the model trained on cosine but queried with L2 produces
nonsense rankings: the classic silent bug, and the reason
"normalized + dot product" is the safe default).

## The scale problem, stated

A billion items as vectors: **a billion points in
300-dimensional space, compared by distance.** The naive
comparison (every query vs every item) is a billion dot products
per query (a 300-billion-FLOP scan: seconds, unusable), and the
entire [ANN](/ai-ml/systems/similarity-search-ann) family
(the next page) exists to answer "the nearest k of a billion" in
milliseconds, trading exactness for speed, the same
[trade](/dsa/patterns/greedy-algorithms) every index makes
([the inverted index](/system-design/fundamentals/search-and-inverted-index)
for terms, the [geo index](/system-design/fundamentals/geospatial-indexing)
for cells, the ANN index for vectors: three instances of one
idea, "organize so you don't compare everything").

## Where embeddings sit in the systems on this site

The embedding is the *representation layer* of the
[recommendation](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system)'s
candidate stage (the two-tower model: user-history embedding,
item embeddings, the ANN index between them), the semantic
half of [hybrid search](/system-design/fundamentals/search-and-inverted-index)
(the term-based inverted index and the vector index, merged:
the modern search stack), the core of every RAG system (the
[LLM-era](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics) retrieval:
documents embedded, queries embedded, the nearest chunks as
context), and the [feature](/ai-ml/systems/feature-engineering-basics)
family for high-cardinality categories (the "merchant" as a vector
instead of a one-hot: the encoding that scales).

## The honest costs

- **The vector's dimensionality is a storage bill** (a billion
  items * 300 dims * 4 bytes = 1.2 TB of vectors, and the
  [quantization](/ai-ml/systems/similarity-search-ann) family
  exists to pay less).
- **The model's era**: embeddings are artifacts (the
  [versioning](/ai-ml/systems/data-versioning-lineage)
  discipline: a new embedding model means *re-embedding
  everything*, which is a migration-scale job, and the reason
  the model choice is made slowly and kept long).
- **The bias of the space**: the embedding encodes its corpus
  (the [fairness](/ai-ml/systems/supervised-unsupervised-rl)
  surface: "similar" inherits the training data's notion of
  similar, and the downstream products
  [drift](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics) with it),
  which is the honest reason the vector is a *system component*
  (versioned, monitored, owned) rather than a library call.
