---
title: "Model Governance & Compliance"
summary: "Audit trails, approval workflows, and the records regulators and reviewers ask for."
---

Model governance is the **organizational discipline** that keeps
deployed models *accountable*: who approved this model, on what
evidence, with what oversight, and, the part that makes it
*engineering*, the **records** that answer those questions after
the fact. It sits at the intersection of the
[responsible-AI](/ai-ml/systems/responsible-ai-safety)
practices (the guardrails and evals), the
[lineage](/ai-ml/systems/data-versioning-lineage) machinery
(the provenance), and the compliance requirements (external, and
getting stricter), and its systems contribution is *making the
governance auditable*: the difference between "we have a review
process" and "here is the record of the review, for this model
version, on this date."

## The artifacts governance produces

- **The model card** (the *documentation* per model: what it does,
  what data it was trained on, its known limitations, its fairness
  metrics, its intended use, the [documentation
  discipline](/system-design/fundamentals/api-design), for models,
  and increasingly a *regulatory requirement* (the EU AI Act's
  high-risk documentation, and the general expectation that a
  model's behavior is *documented* before deployment).
- **The approval record** (who signed off, on what evidence: the
  [eval results](/ai-ml/systems/evaluating-llm-systems), the
  [fairness audit](/ai-ml/systems/bias-fairness-explainability),
  the risk assessment, the [change-management](/system-design/fundamentals/reliability-engineering)
  record, model-shaped, and the *human gate* the
  [responsible-AI](/ai-ml/systems/responsible-ai-safety) page
  requires for high-stakes models).
- **The lineage** (the full provenance: data, code, config,
  [reproducibility](/ai-ml/systems/reproducibility-metadata)
  metadata, the *technical* record governance *depends on*, which
  is why governance and MLOps are not separable: governance without
  lineage is paperwork).
- **The monitoring record** (the model's *ongoing* behavior: the
  [drift](/ai-ml/systems/model-monitoring-drift) reports, the
  fairness monitoring, the incidents, because governance is not a
  *launch* gate, it is a *standing* obligation: a model that was
  fair at launch and biased a year later is a governance failure).
- **The decision log** (which model scored which decision, with
  what explanation: the [fraud-detection
  pattern](/system-design/hld/fraud-detection), generalized, the
  record that answers "why did the system decide this about me",
  the [explainability](/ai-ml/systems/bias-fairness-explainability)
  requirement, *retained*).

## The systems shape

Governance is a **process over the [platform](/ai-ml/systems/mlops-cicd)
machinery**, and its engineering is *enforcement*: the
[model registry](/ai-ml/systems/model-registry)'s stage
transitions gain *gates* (a model cannot move to `production`
without an approved model card and a passing audit: the
[CI/CD gate](/ai-ml/systems/mlops-cicd), policy-shaped), the
[lineage](/ai-ml/systems/data-versioning-lineage) and
[decision logs](/system-design/hld/fraud-detection) are *retained*
per policy (years, the [object-storage
lifecycle](/system-design/fundamentals/object-storage)), and the
*approval workflow* (the human gate) is a
[ticket/workflow](/system-design/hld/distributed-job-scheduler)
system, integrated so that "approved" is a *state the pipeline
checks*, not a document someone filed. The design principle: **make
the governance *executable*** (a gate the pipeline enforces),
because governance that lives in a separate document is governance
that drifts from reality the moment the pipeline moves fast.

## Why it is growing (and not going away)

The regulatory environment is tightening (the EU AI Act, sector
rules (lending, hiring, health), and the general expectation that
automated decisions are *explainable and appealable*), which means
governance moves from "best practice" to "requirement" for a
growing set of models, and the engineering that makes it
*survivable* (not a bottleneck) is exactly the machinery above:
lineage that is *already recorded* for reproducibility (governance
*reads* it), model cards generated *from* the metadata (not written
by hand), audits that are *queries* over the registry (not manual
reviews), and gates that are *automated checks* (not meetings).
The teams that treat governance as *paperwork* find it slows them
down; the teams that treat it as *records already produced by a
good platform* find it nearly free, which is the page's point.
