---
title: "Feature Engineering Basics"
summary: "Turning raw events into model inputs: the transforms, the leakage traps, and the training/serving consistency that makes features real."
---

A model cannot learn from an event log; it learns from **features**,
the numeric summaries, the categorical encodings, the derived
signals that a human (or a pipeline) decided were *informative*.
Feature engineering is that transformation, and it is the part of ML
where human judgment survives the deepest into production: a good
feature (7-day spend, distinct merchant count,
[text-embedded](/ai-ml/systems/embeddings-vector-representations)
title) is worth more than a modeling breakthrough, and a bad one
(a leak, a skew) poisons everything downstream
([the training pipeline](/ai-ml/systems/training-data-pipelines)'s
central hazard). This page is the engineering of features: the
transform families, the two deadly traps, and the consistency
discipline that makes a feature *deployable*.

## The transform families

The vocabulary, worth having because each family answers a different
data shape:

- **Aggregations over time** (the workhorse:
  [windowed](/system-design/fundamentals/stream-vs-batch-processing)
  counts and sums per entity: "7-day spend", "logins per hour",
  "distinct merchants in 24h", the
  [fraud features](/system-design/hld/fraud-detection) verbatim).
  The design decisions: window size (too short: noisy; too long:
  stale), and **point-in-time** (the window computed *as of the
  event*, not as of now: the training/serving contract).
- **Counts and ratios** (frequency signals: "how often does this
  user do X", normalized by their own baseline: ratios beat raw
  counts, because users have different volumes, the same
  [per-tenant](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
  normalization that rate limiting uses).
- **Categorical encodings** (turning "merchant = Uber" into
  numbers): one-hot (fine for few values),
  [target/statistics encodings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_encoding)
  (the category's historical outcome rate: powerful, and a leakage
  trap if computed including the current example, the encoding
  must be fit on *past* data only), and
  [embeddings](/ai-ml/systems/embeddings-vector-representations)
  (learned dense vectors: the modern answer for high-cardinality
  categories).
- **Text → vectors** (the
  [embedding](/ai-ml/systems/embeddings-vector-representations)
  family: titles, descriptions, and queries become geometry), and
  **geospatial** ([cells](/system-design/fundamentals/geospatial-indexing)
  and distances as features).
- **Cross features** (combinations: "this user's *category*
  engagement rate", the personalization signal, and the
  [interaction features](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system)
  the ranker consumes).

## Trap one: leakage, the complete listing

[A training example](/ai-ml/systems/training-data-pipelines)
must look exactly like a request will look at prediction time, and
every feature that violates this is **leakage** (the model learns
from information it will not have, evaluates perfectly, and fails
in production). The complete family, because each is a classic:

- **Label leakage** (the feature *is* the answer, or encodes it:
  "days since last chargeback" predicting chargebacks).
- **Future leakage** (the feature computed over data that includes
  the prediction's future: aggregates "as of now" instead of "as of
  the event").
- **Encoding leakage** ([target encodings](#the-transform-families)
  fit on the full dataset: the current example's label inside its
  own category's average, the fix is *out-of-fold* computation,
  the same discipline as the held-out split).
- **Serving leakage** (a feature that *cannot exist* online: a
  "full-dataset" statistic, a join that requires the outcome, a
  computation over more data than the
  [latency budget](/system-design/fundamentals/latency-vs-throughput)
  allows, the model trains on it, the serving path cannot compute
  it, and the feature silently degrades to garbage at deploy).

The detector, worth drilling: **suspiciously good offline metrics**
are leakage until proven otherwise (a 99.9%-accurate fraud model is
not a discovery), and the systematic check is the
*point-in-time replay*: recompute the feature pipeline "as of a past
date" and confirm the metrics match what the training set claimed.

## Trap two: training/serving skew

The second deadly trap, and the quiet one: **the same feature, two
implementations**, the batch pipeline computes "7-day spend" one
way (calendar days, warehouse joins, midnight-clocked), the serving
path computes it another (trailing hours,
[feature-store](/ai-ml/systems/feature-stores) lookups,
request-time-clocked), and the model's inputs at serving are
*different data* than it trained on. The model is then
[drifting](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics) not
because the world changed, but because *the code did*.

The discipline: **one definition, two executions** (the feature
defined once, a versioned definition, the same
[lineage](/ai-ml/systems/data-versioning-lineage) treatment
as data, and executed by the batch trainer and the online server
from the same source), which is exactly what a
[feature store](/ai-ml/systems/feature-stores) exists to
enforce (one computation, materialized offline for training and
served online for prediction: the *store* as the consistency
guarantee), and the honest admission that the *store* is the
standard answer because the *alternative* (discipline alone)
fails at scale.

## The craft, briefly

The judgment half (what makes a *good* feature), in the
engineering-usable form:

- **Domain knowledge beats volume** (the fraud signal "payment
  two hours after card added" is worth a hundred generic
  aggregates: features encode hypotheses, and the hypothesis
  quality is the ceiling).
- **The model is free to ignore features** (adding candidates is
  cheap; the pipeline's job is computing them consistently, the
  model's job is weighting them, so generate generously, and let
  [evaluation](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics)
  prune).
- **Stability over cleverness** (a feature that behaves the same
  way across [data eras](/ai-ml/systems/data-labeling-annotation)
  and traffic shapes is worth more than a sharper one that
  doesn't: the [drift](/ai-ml/systems/ml-system-design-basics)
  surface area scales with feature count, which is the real cost
  of "generate generously").
