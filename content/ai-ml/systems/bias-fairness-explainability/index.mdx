---
title: "Bias, Fairness & Explainability"
summary: "Measuring disparate impact, and making a model's decisions legible to the people they affect."
---

A model learns whatever its data encodes, and its data encodes the
world's existing patterns — including the ones society has decided
are unacceptable. **Fairness** is the discipline of measuring *whose
outcomes differ* and deciding whether the difference is
justified; **explainability** is the discipline of making a
decision *legible* to the person it affects (and to the regulator
asking why). Both are *systems* topics because both are
requirements on the pipeline (which data, which features, which
monitoring) and on the serving path (which reasons are produced,
which decisions are reviewable), not properties a model has or
lacks in the abstract.

## The fairness definitions, and their impossibility

The measurement vocabulary — and the reason fairness is *hard*,
not just *underspecified*: several intuitive definitions are
**mutually incompatible** (the
[impossibility theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairness_(machine_learning)#Definitions))
except in degenerate cases, so "make it fair" requires *choosing
which fairness*:

- **Demographic parity**: the positive-decision rate is equal
  across groups (a hiring model selects men and women at the same
  rate). Ignores base rates entirely, which is why it can demand
  *unfair* outcomes when groups genuinely differ.
- **Equal opportunity**: the true-positive rate is equal across
  groups (qualified applicants from each group are accepted at the
  same rate). The "equal treatment of the qualified" reading.
- **Equalized odds**: both true-positive and false-positive rates
  equal. Stronger, and often infeasible alongside the others.
- **Individual fairness / calibration**: similar individuals get
  similar decisions; or scores mean the same thing across groups.

The design consequence, and the honest thing to say out loud: **you
cannot satisfy all of them at once**, so the *product* and *legal*
context must pick — which is a *governance* decision (the
[responsible-AI](/ai-ml/systems/responsible-ai-safety) page's
territory), encoded as a metric the system is measured against,
audited, and monitored. "We chose equal opportunity, and here is the
audit" is a defensible answer; "we did not choose" is not.

## Where bias enters (the pipeline view)

Bias is not one bug; it is a family, one per pipeline stage, and
each needs its own mitigation:

- **Data collection**: the sample is not the population
  (the training data over-represents some groups: the
  [labeling](/ai-ml/systems/data-labeling-annotation) and
  [logged-outcome](/ai-ml/systems/supervised-unsupervised-rl)
  biases), and the **historical labels encode historical
  discrimination** (a hiring model trained on biased past hires
  learns the bias: the "the label *is* the problem" case).
- **Feature choice**: **proxy features** (zip code, name,
  browsing history: correlated with protected attributes without
  naming them), the classic and legally fraught case, requiring
  the feature-audit discipline.
- **Label bias**: the annotators' biases, the
  [delayed labels'](/ai-ml/systems/data-labeling-annotation)
  selection effects (only the escalated cases get labeled: the
  [feedback loop](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system) again).
- **Evaluation and deployment**: the *aggregate metric hiding the
  segment* (the [evaluation
  trap](/ai-ml/systems/evaluation-metrics): "95% accurate"
  while the minority class is at 60%), which is why **per-segment
  metric breakdowns are the first fairness tool**, before any
  algorithmic intervention.

## The interventions, and their costs

- **Pre-processing** (fix the data: reweighting, resampling,
  removing proxy features): cheap, upstream, and limited (you can
  only remove bias the data *contains*).
- **In-processing** (constrain the training: fairness
  regularizers, adversarial debiasing): effective, opaque, and the
  hardest to explain to a regulator.
- **Post-processing** (adjust the *thresholds per group*: the
  direct way to hit equal-opportunity, at the cost of an explicit
  group-based rule, which is itself legally fraught in some
  jurisdictions — the honest tension the design must surface, not
  hide).

The systems point: the intervention's *placement* determines its
auditability, and the audit is often the actual requirement
(the model must be *explainable to the regulator*, not merely
fair).

## Explainability: two needs, two tools

- **Global explanations** ("what does this model rely on overall?"):
  feature importances, SHAP summaries, the surrogate-model
  approach — for the model owner, the reviewer, and the
  governance record.
- **Local explanations** ("why *this* decision?"): the
  [fraud](/system-design/hld/fraud-detection) block's reason code,
  the credit denial's adverse-action notice (a *legal requirement*
  in lending: the specific reasons), the "because you watched"
  [surface](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system) — for the
  affected person, and this is the one that is a *product and legal
  requirement*, not a nice-to-have.
- **Inherently interpretable models** (linear models, small trees,
  rule lists): the alternative strategy — *choose a model you can
  explain*, accepting lower accuracy, which for high-stakes,
  regulated decisions (credit, hiring, parole) is often the correct
  trade and the reason those domains still run
  [scorecards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_score) and
  GBDTs rather than the largest model available.

## The systems design (what this page actually asks you to build)

- **Segment-level evaluation, first-class** (the metric pipeline
  computes every metric *per protected segment*, the same
  [multi-tenant](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
  breakdown discipline, and the
  [monitoring](/ai-ml/systems/model-monitoring-drift) alerts
  on *segment* regressions, not just aggregate).
- **Feature auditing** (a review gate on features with protected
  correlation: the [lineage](/ai-ml/systems/data-versioning-lineage)
  record makes "what does this model use" answerable).
- **Explanation production** (the serving path emits reasons, not
  just scores: the [fraud](/system-design/hld/fraud-detection)
  reason codes, the [recommendation](/system-design/hld/recommendation-system)
  sources — the [API contract](/system-design/fundamentals/api-design)
  carries them, and the explanation's *provenance* is part of the
  decision log).
- **The governance loop** (the fairness metric chosen, audited,
  reported, and revisited: the
  [responsible-AI](/ai-ml/systems/responsible-ai-safety)
  machinery, and the honest admission that this is a *standing
  process*, not a pre-launch checkbox).
