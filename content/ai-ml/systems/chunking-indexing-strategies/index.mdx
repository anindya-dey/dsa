title: "Chunking & Indexing Strategies"
summary: "How documents become retrievable chunks, and why chunking decides RAG's quality."

The unglamorous stage that decides [RAG's](/ai-ml/systems/rag)
quality more than any model choice: **chunking**, splitting
documents into the pieces that get embedded and retrieved. The
design tension is exact and inescapable: **chunks must be small
enough to be specific** (a huge chunk embeds a blur of many topics,
so its vector matches nothing precisely) **and large enough to be
self-contained** (a tiny chunk loses the context that makes it
meaningful: "as shown above" with no above). Every chunking
strategy is a position on that dial, and every RAG retrieval failure
traces back to it.

## The chunking strategies

- **Fixed-size** (split every N tokens, with overlap): the naive
  baseline, simple, fast, and *semantically blind* (it splits
  mid-sentence, mid-table, mid-thought). The overlap (say 10-20%)
  mitigates the worst splits, and it is a *starting point*, not an
  answer.
- **Recursive/structural** (split on the document's *own*
  structure: paragraphs, then sentences, then tokens, Markdown
  headings, HTML sections, code blocks): the standard improvement,
  because the document's structure *is* a semantic boundary, a
  section is a coherent unit, and splitting on it keeps the
  coherence.
- **Semantic chunking** (split where the *meaning* shifts: embed
  sentences, break where consecutive sentences diverge, the
  embedding-space discontinuity as the boundary): more accurate,
  more expensive (an embedding pass over the whole corpus), and
  the modern option.
- **Document-aware** (tables kept whole, code kept whole, headings
  *prepended* to their chunks: "this chunk is from the *Billing*
  section of the *Refunds* doc", the **contextual enrichment**
  that makes a chunk self-contained without making it huge, and a
  big practical win: a chunk that knows its own heading retrieves
  far better).

The design's honest default, for most corpora: **structural chunking
with heading-enrichment and modest overlap**, tuned against the
[eval set](/ai-ml/systems/evaluating-llm-systems), not chosen
once and forgotten.

## The indexing decisions around chunking

- **The embedding model** (which [vector
  space](/ai-ml/systems/embeddings-vector-representations) the
  chunks land in: a *corpus-specific* choice, a legal corpus wants
  a legal-domain embedding, and the
  [era-migration](/ai-ml/systems/vector-databases) cost of
  changing it is the reason the choice is deliberate).
- **Metadata per chunk** (source document, section, page,
  timestamp, permissions: what enables *filtering* (only this
  tenant's docs), *citations* (the user sees where it came from),
  and *freshness* (the newest version), the
  [vector-database's](/ai-ml/systems/vector-databases)
  payload, and the reason chunking and metadata are one design).
- **Hybrid indexing** (the chunk is embedded *and* its text is
  keyword-indexed: the [hybrid
  search](/system-design/fundamentals/search-and-inverted-index)
  the [RAG page](/ai-ml/systems/rag) recommends, a chunk
  retrievable by meaning *and* by exact term, merged).
- **The re-indexing pipeline** (documents change: a standing
  [pipeline](/system-design/fundamentals/stream-vs-batch-processing)
  that re-chunks and re-embeds the *changed* documents (not the
  whole corpus: incremental, the [CDC](/system-design/fundamentals/olap-vs-oltp)
  shape), invalidates the old chunks, and keeps the index fresh,
the reason a RAG system is a *standing* system, and the
  [freshness](/system-design/fundamentals/caching) contract that
  makes "the answer is stale" a pipeline bug, not a model one).

## Why chunking dominates quality

The reason this stage is worth its own page: **retrieval can only
return what the chunking produced.** If the answer spans two chunks,
neither is retrieved as the answer. If a chunk is too big, its vector
is a blur and matches nothing precisely. If a chunk lacks its
heading, it is ambiguous ("the limit is $500", *which* limit?).
The [RAG failure taxonomy](/ai-ml/systems/rag) is, stage by
stage, a *chunking* critique, and the practical wisdom every RAG
team learns: **improve the chunking before you improve the model**,
because retrieval is the bottleneck and chunking is where retrieval
is decided.
