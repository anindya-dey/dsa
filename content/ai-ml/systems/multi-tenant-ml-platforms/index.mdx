---
title: "Multi-Tenant ML Platforms"
summary: "Sharing training and serving infrastructure across teams and customers, safely."
---

A multi-tenant ML platform serves *many consumers* — internal teams
(each with its own models and data), or external customers (each
with its own fine-tuned models and private data) — from *shared*
infrastructure: shared [GPUs](/ai-ml/systems/gpu-accelerator-management),
shared [serving fleets](/ai-ml/systems/multi-model-serving-routing),
shared [pipelines](/ai-ml/systems/mlops-cicd). The reason it
is hard is the same reason all multi-tenancy is hard
([the multi-tenancy page](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)),
amplified by ML's resources: the tenants share *scarce, expensive*
accelerators, their data is often *sensitive*, and the workloads
are *bursty and unequal* (one team's training run can starve
everyone's serving). This is the platform-engineering synthesis of
the whole AI/ML track: everything above, shared, with isolation.

## The isolation dimensions

Each resource the tenants share, and its isolation mechanism:

- **Compute (GPUs)** (the scarce resource: isolation via
  [scheduling](/ai-ml/systems/gpu-accelerator-management)
  (quotas per team, priority classes, preemption: the batch
  training yields to the latency-sensitive serving), and the
  *sharing* trade (MIG/MPS partitioning: packing vs. isolation, the
  [accelerator page's](/ai-ml/systems/gpu-accelerator-management)
  dial) — the fairness problem (one team's run must not starve
  another's serving) is the [noisy
  neighbor](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency),
  at GPU scale).
- **Models** (the serving fleet hosts many teams' models: isolation
  via the [multi-model routing](/ai-ml/systems/multi-model-serving-routing)
  (each tenant's requests route to *their* models) and the
  [warm-pool](/ai-ml/systems/model-caching-warm-pools)
  *residency* policy (a tenant's hot model is warm; a tenant's cold
  model is not, and *their* traffic pattern decides) — plus the
  *fairness* of the fleet's capacity (one tenant's viral model must
  not evict another's production model from the warm pool: the
  [cache-isolation](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
  problem).
- **Data** (the *sensitive* dimension: each tenant's training data,
  features, and models are *private* — isolation via the
  [storage/authz](/system-design/fundamentals/security-fundamentals)
  discipline (per-tenant buckets, row-level security, scoped
  credentials) and the *training* isolation (a tenant's job reads
  only *their* data: the [least-privilege](/system-design/fundamentals/security-fundamentals)
  rule, enforced at the pipeline), and — for external customers —
  the [residency](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
  constraint (their data may not leave their region).
- **Pipelines and registries** (the shared [MLOps](/ai-ml/systems/mlops-cicd)
  machinery: each tenant's runs, models, and lineage are *scoped*
  (their registry namespace, their experiment tracker project), and
  the *shared* platform code (the pipeline templates) is what makes
  the platform a platform (build once, every tenant uses it).
- **Cost** (the platform's bill, attributed *per tenant*: the
  [cost-optimization](/ai-ml/systems/cost-optimization-ml)
  discipline, with the *chargeback/showback* question — internal
  teams pay their share, external customers are billed — which
  *drives* the utilization behavior (a team that pays for idle GPUs
  stops wasting them): the [unit-economics](/system-design/fundamentals/capacity-planning-and-estimation)
  metric, per tenant).

## The platform's shape

A multi-tenant ML platform is, structurally: a **control plane**
(tenant management, quotas, the registry/tracker namespaces, the
approval workflows: the [metadata
plane](/system-design/hld/object-storage-system)) over a **shared
resource plane** (the GPU scheduler, the serving fleet, the storage
and pipelines), with the **isolation** enforced at the seams
(authz on data, routing on models, quotas on compute) — which is
the [multi-tenancy](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
architecture, at ML scale, with the GPU as the shared scarce
resource that makes it *interesting*.

## The tenancy models, ML-shaped

The [multi-tenancy page's](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
spectrum, applied here:

- **Shared everything** (one fleet, one cluster, all tenants
  sharing GPUs, models, and pipelines, isolated by quotas and
  routing): maximum efficiency, maximum blast radius (a platform
  bug is *every* tenant's bug), the internal-platform default.
- **Shared control plane, isolated data plane** (the common
  production shape: one platform, per-tenant serving/model/data
  isolation: the efficiency of shared ops with the safety of
  isolated compute) — and the *external-customer* answer, where
  isolation is *contractual* (a customer's model must not share a
  GPU with a competitor's, for trust reasons even when technically
  safe).
- **Dedicated tiers** (the enterprise SKU: a customer pays for
  isolation: the [multi-tenancy page's](/system-design/fundamentals/multi-tenancy-and-data-residency)
  dedicated model, and the ML version — dedicated GPUs, dedicated
  model residency, dedicated pipelines).
